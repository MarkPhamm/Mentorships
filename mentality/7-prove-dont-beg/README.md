# 7. Prove, don't beg

## School was a different game

In high school, if they picked the top 30 students by score, everyone could make it. Your score didn't take anything away from mine. And there was a clear benchmark: tests measured your maths, your English, your subjects. None of them measured how you looked, how tall you were, your accent, or how good you were at making people like you.

## The real world isn't

Two things change after school.

**People are judging creatures.** Most of us are, whether we admit it or not. Interviewers notice how you dress, how you speak, how you carry yourself and whether they'd enjoy working with you, alongside what you know. It isn't fair, and it's how it works. Control what you can: show up prepared, dress the part, speak clearly, and be someone people want in the room.

**It's a zero-sum game.** There's one offer. If you get it, someone else doesn't. There's no curve where everyone passes. And there's no objective scale either, just a group of people deciding who they believe in most.

So it comes down to **who works harder and who works smarter**, and who can make the people in the room believe it.

## Prove, don't beg

You have to prove to them that you're good enough and bright enough. **Prove it. Don't beg for it.**

People smell desperation faster than anything else (see [Detach](../3-detachment/README.md)). That's why I believe the key to a successful interview comes down to one thing: **confidence.** Not arrogance: confidence that comes from having done the work, and knowing it.

Change the frame. You're not asking them for a job. **You're showing them you're the best person for it, and if they reject you, they're the ones missing out.**

Watch Harvey Specter interviewing Mike Ross in the first episode of *Suits*. Mike walks in with no law degree, no Harvard, nothing on paper, and wins the job anyway. He doesn't plead his case. He shows Harvey what he can do, and makes Harvey realise he'd be a fool to pass on him. That's the energy.

## Desperate posts are a red flag

You've seen the LinkedIn posts: *"I've been applying for 8 months and I'm losing hope. Please, any opportunity, I'll do anything."*

I understand the feeling behind them. But to a hiring manager, a post like that is a huge red flag. It doesn't say "hire me". It says **"nobody wants me."** And nobody wants to be the first to take a chance on someone everybody else has passed on.

| Begging | Proving |
|---|---|
| "Still looking for my first data role, any help appreciated 🙏" | "I scraped 160K airline reviews and modelled them in dbt. Here's the one thing that surprised me." |
| "Please, I'll accept any role, any salary." | "Here's the pipeline I built this month, and what I'd do differently next time." |
| "Day 200 of job hunting…" | "What I learned building my first CDC pipeline with Postgres and Fivetran." |

Every post, message and interview answer either proves something or begs for something. Make it prove.

---

## Questions to ask yourself

- [ ] Am I still thinking like it's school, where doing well enough means everyone passes?
- [ ] What can I control about how I come across (preparation, clothes, posture, speaking) that I'm not working on?
- [ ] In my last interview, was I proving or asking?
- [ ] Would my last LinkedIn post make a hiring manager want me, or pity me?
