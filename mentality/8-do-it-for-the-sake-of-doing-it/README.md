# 8. Do it for the sake of doing it

Do things out of curiosity, and help people, even when it's free. **It pays out later**, in ways you can't plan for.

## I never once asked for anything

- I **write for free:** my [Substack](https://substack.com/@markphammm) and my posts in [Xóm Data](https://www.facebook.com/groups/xomdata).
- I **collaborate with Vu Trinh for free** on [vutr](https://vutr.substack.com).
- I **help Tam Mai for free.**
- I **review resumes for free** every week in Xóm Data.
- I **mentor**, and this whole repo is free.

None of it came with an invoice or a "you owe me one". And all of it came back in some form: the skills I learned writing, the people who know my work, the friends and colleagues who vouch for me, the community I get to be part of. I didn't plan any of that. I just kept doing the work.

## Fall in love with the process, not the result

The moment you do something *for* the outcome, it gets worse, and so do you.

- **Mentor for the sake of helping people,** not for the money.
- **Write for the sake of writing and learning,** not for the views. Writing something down is the fastest way to find out whether you really understand it.
- **Build community because you love connecting people,** not because you want something from them. People can tell the difference immediately.
- **Build projects because you're curious** how something works, not only because a recruiter might look at them.

When the process is the reward, you don't need the result to keep going. That's also the purest form of [detachment](../3-detachment/README.md): you can't be desperate for an outcome you weren't doing it for.

## Be obsessed

When you're obsessed with something, time flies. You look up and it's 2 a.m. and you're still reading about how Parquet stores data, not because anyone asked you to, but because you wanted to know. That's when you learn fastest, because you're not forcing it.

Obsession is also what makes [pushing to failure](../5-push-to-failure/README.md) sustainable. Grinding through something you hate burns you out. Grinding through something you love doesn't feel like grinding.

## How to start

- **Write down what you learned this week** and post it. A short post is enough. You'll learn it twice: once doing it, once explaining it.
- **Answer one question a week** in a community you're part of.
- **Help one person** a little behind you: review a resume, explain a concept, share your project.
- **Open-source the thing you built,** with a README good enough that someone else can use it.
- **Don't keep score.** The moment you start counting what people owe you, it stops working.

(One line I'd draw: this is about helping people and creating things, not doing a company's paid work for free.)

---

## Questions to ask yourself

- [ ] What would I keep doing even if nobody paid me or watched?
- [ ] When did I last help someone without expecting anything back?
- [ ] Am I writing, building or mentoring for the process, or for the numbers?
- [ ] When did I last lose track of time because I was curious about something?
