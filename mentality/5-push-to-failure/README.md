# 5. Push to failure

Picture a guy walking into the gym for the first time, out of shape. Every session, he picks a moderate weight, does 10 comfortable reps, puts it down and goes home. He's there four times a week. He'll never get in shape.

The guy who changes is the one who **pushes until he can't do another rep, then rests.** The growth happens in those last reps, the ones that feel impossible. Everything before them is a warm-up.

Work is exactly the same. **The more you push, the further and faster you go.**

## What "10 comfortable reps" looks like at work

- Solving the 50th easy SQL problem instead of attempting the first hard one.
- Doing another tutorial instead of building something that might break.
- Taking the tickets you already know how to do.
- Stopping at "it works" instead of making it fast, tested and documented.
- Applying only to jobs you're overqualified for, because rejection from the others would sting.

None of this is wrong. It just doesn't make you better. It keeps you exactly where you are.

## How to train to failure

- **Find your moderate weight.** What do you do every week that no longer challenges you? That's the weight you've outgrown.
- **Add load every week.** One harder problem, one unfamiliar tool, one ticket you're not sure you can finish. Lifters call it progressive overload: a little more than last time, every time.
- **Go until you actually can't,** not until it gets uncomfortable. Uncomfortable is where it starts working.
- **Then rest, properly.** Sleep, eat, get away from the screen. Rest is part of the program, not the opposite of it. (And it's not the same as quitting; see [part 6](../6-no-such-thing-as-burnout/README.md).)
- **Track your reps.** Problems solved, things shipped, applications sent, interviews done. You can't push a number you don't measure.

## Failed reps are the point

In the gym, the rep you fail is the one that tells you where your limit is, and that's exactly where you need to be training. A rejection, a bug you can't fix, an interview question you bombed: those are failed reps. They mean you were working at your limit.

**Resilience is just getting back under the bar.** Not taking a week off because one rep failed. Not dropping back to a lighter weight to feel good again. Rest, then go again.

---

## Questions to ask yourself

- [ ] What's my "moderate weight" right now: the work I keep doing because it's comfortable?
- [ ] What's one thing this week that I'm genuinely not sure I can do?
- [ ] When did I last push something until I really couldn't do more?
- [ ] After my last failed rep (rejection, bug, bad interview), how fast did I get back under the bar?
