# 0. Apply the way you swipe on Tinder

## The market is congested

There are far too many applicants and not nearly enough employers. One entry-level data posting can pull in thousands of applications within days. You are not the one being chased. **You're one of many people trying to get noticed.**

It's a lot like dating apps. The numbers are lopsided: women get flooded with likes, while most men swipe and swipe and get very few matches back. **In this job market, you're the guy.** Act accordingly.

## Swipe a lot

The guy who swipes ten times a week and waits for love to find him stays single. **The more you swipe, the better your chance of getting a match.**

- **Apply in volume.** Don't spend a week polishing one application and then wait for an answer. Keep the pipeline full.
- **You don't have to be sure you want it.** You can say no after the first date. An interview is a first date: you learn about them while they learn about you. If you don't like what you see, decline then. Don't pre-reject yourself by never applying.
- **Keep swiping while you're dating.** One interview in progress isn't a reason to stop applying. Plenty of first dates go nowhere. Options keep you calm ([Detach](../3-detachment/README.md)).
- **Volume, with a floor.** Swipe on roles you're a plausible match for, not every posting on the site. Spending 30 seconds checking that the role is in your field and roughly your level is what separates swiping from spamming.

## Fire and forget

Be honest: you don't remember who you swiped on yesterday. Unless she was stunning, and then you didn't just swipe, you used a **Super Like**.

Apply the same way:

- **Most applications are swipes. Fire and forget.** Don't spend an hour on each one, don't keep thinking about it afterwards, and don't refresh your inbox waiting for an answer. Send it, log it in your spreadsheet, move on to the next.
- **The few roles you really want get a Super Like.** Those are worth real effort:
  - **Tailor your resume** to that job description: reorder your bullets, mirror their keywords, lead with what they care about ([resume guide](../../resume/README.md#tailor-for-the-roles-you-really-want)).
  - **Ask for a referral** from someone you know there.
  - **Reach out to the team** and learn their stack before the interview.

| | Swipe | Super Like |
|---|---|---|
| How many | Most of your applications | Your top 5–10 roles |
| Time per application | A few minutes | Hours |
| Resume | Your strong general version | Tailored to their job description |
| Referral | Only if you happen to have one | Always try to get one |
| After you apply | Forget about it | Follow up, prepare, research the team |

Swipes keep the pipeline full. Super Likes are where you win the roles that matter most. Don't mix them up: a Super Like on everything is the same as a Super Like on nothing, and you'll spread yourself too thin to stand out on the roles you care about.

## Your resume is your Tinder profile

Someone gives it **about 5 seconds** before deciding left or right. So put your best stuff where they'll see it first:

- Your strongest, most specific achievement at the top, not buried in the third bullet of your second job.
- Your [competitive advantage](../4-competitive-advantage/README.md) up front.
- Nothing that makes them hesitate: typos, broken links, an empty GitHub, a photo that looks like it was taken in a car.

The full checklists are in the [resume](../../resume/README.md), [LinkedIn](../../linkedin/README.md), [GitHub](../../github/README.md) and [portfolio](../../portfolio/README.md) guides.

## No matches? Fix the profile

If you've swiped a lot and nothing's coming back, the market isn't the only problem. Your profile is. You're a data person, so treat it like a funnel:

| Stage | If it's low… | …fix this |
|---|---|---|
| Applications → replies | Your profile isn't getting right-swiped | Resume and LinkedIn: the first 5 seconds. Is your best stuff at the top? Does it pass the [copy-paste test](../1-dont-be-a-minion/README.md)? |
| Replies → interviews | You get a look but not a date | Fit and targeting. Are you applying at the right level? Does your resume match the job's keywords? |
| Interviews → offers | The dates go badly | Interview prep, confidence ([Prove, don't beg](../7-prove-dont-beg/README.md)) and how specifically you've prepared for that team ([the Lazard story](../4-competitive-advantage/README.md#how-i-got-into-lazard)) |

Track your numbers in a spreadsheet: where you applied, when, and how far you got. After 30–50 applications you'll know which stage is broken, and you can fix that instead of guessing.

## A good introduction beats a hundred swipes

All in all, the best way to meet someone is still through a friend. A **referral** skips the pile: a real person vouching for you means your profile gets read instead of skimmed.

- **Ask people you already know** who work at companies you like. Most companies make it easy for employees to refer.
- **Talk to people on the team before you apply** ([the Lazard story](../4-competitive-advantage/README.md#how-i-got-into-lazard)).
- **Help people before you need them** ([Do it for the sake of doing it](../8-do-it-for-the-sake-of-doing-it/README.md)). The introductions come from the relationships you built when you weren't asking for anything.
- **Make it easy for them:** send a short note on why you're a fit, your resume, and the link to the role.

Swipe a lot, keep your profile sharp, fix it when the numbers tell you to, and get introduced whenever you can.

## Questions to ask yourself

- [ ] How many applications did I send in the last two weeks? Is that swiping, or waiting?
- [ ] Have I pre-rejected myself from roles I'd at least want a first date with?
- [ ] Which 2–3 roles are my Super Likes? Have I tailored my resume and asked for a referral for each?
- [ ] Am I spending Super Like effort on swipes, or worrying about applications I should have forgotten?
- [ ] Do I track my funnel? Which stage is my weakest?
- [ ] What does someone see in the first 5 seconds of my resume?
- [ ] Who do I know at the 5 companies I want most, and have I asked them for an introduction?
