# 10. Be proactive: be a little extra

In every stage of your career, attitude always plays a crucial role. However, being **"a little extra" proactive in the early stages always gives you more advantage.**

Early on, nobody expects much from you. You're the intern, the junior, the new grad. That's exactly why a little extra stands out so much: the bar is low, so clearing it by a bit is visible to everyone. Ten years in, doing more than asked is just the job. In your first year, it's how people remember your name.

## What "a little extra" looks like

Not working 80 hours. Not saying yes to everything. Small things, done before anyone asks:

- **Don't wait for the ticket.** If you see a broken test, a missing doc or a dashboard nobody trusts, flag it and offer to fix it. "I noticed X, I can take it" is the most useful sentence a junior can say.
- **Ask before you're stuck for a day.** Try first, then ask with what you tried. Being proactive includes asking early, not suffering in silence to look independent.
- **Follow up.** After an interview, a coffee chat, a meeting. Most people don't, so the ones who do get remembered.
- **Close the loop.** When you finish something, say so, with the result. Don't make your manager chase you for status.
- **Learn the next layer before you need it.** If you're an analyst, read the dbt models behind your dashboard. If you're in dbt, read the ingestion code. You'll be ready when the opportunity shows up.
- **Reach out first.** Message the person whose post you liked, the engineer on the team you want to join, the mentor you want. The worst answer is no reply, and that costs you nothing.

## In the job search

The same attitude applies before you're hired:

- **Build before anyone asks you to.** A project nobody assigned is proof of initiative. See the [projects guide](../../projects/README.md).
- **Apply and reach out at the same time.** Don't send an application and wait. Find someone on the team and introduce yourself. See [Apply the way you swipe on Tinder](../0-apply-like-tinder/README.md).
- **Show up prepared.** Read about the company, its data stack and the interviewer before the call. Bring a question nobody else would ask.

## Extra, not desperate

Proactive is not the same as desperate. A proactive person offers value: "I noticed this, here's a fix." A desperate person asks for reassurance: "Please, any update?" The first one shows you have options and energy to spare; the second shows you have neither. If it starts to feel like begging, go back to [Detach](../3-detachment/README.md) and [Prove, don't beg](../7-prove-dont-beg/README.md).
