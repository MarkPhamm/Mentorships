# 2. Play it like poker

A job search isn't an exam where the right answer gets full marks. It's a game of poker. Everyone at the table is presenting the strongest version of themselves, and **you can say whatever you want, as long as you can back it up.** Don't blame the player, blame the game.

Sometimes you have to inflate yourself to look more impressive. Inflate too much, and someone calls your bluff. There are four ways to lose:

- **If you don't bluff, you lose.** You write "assisted with dashboards" when you built them yourself, and the candidate who wrote "built" gets the call.
- **If you bluff too much, you lose.** You claim you "architected the data platform" and the first follow-up question exposes you. Now nothing else you said is believed either.
- **If you play it too safe, you lose.** Duties instead of wins, every number rounded down, everything hedged with "helped" and "supported".
- **If you're too risky, you lose too.** Claims you can't explain, numbers you can't derive, tools you've only read about.

**Find your sweet spot.** The test: could you defend this claim for 10 minutes while a senior engineer drills into it? If yes, play it with confidence: the strongest verb and the most generous number you can explain. If no, pull it back until you can.

## Cards that are always face up

Some cards can't be bluffed, because anyone can check them: **employers, job titles, dates, degrees, GPA and certifications.** Background checks verify them, and recruiters compare them with your LinkedIn. Bluff on how you frame your work, never on these facts. Get caught here and you don't just lose the hand: offers get rescinded.

## It's not just the resume

The same game runs through the whole process:

- **In interviews:** talk about your work like you own it. "We" for the team's work, "I" for yours, and know exactly which is which.
- **When you don't know something:** don't fake it. Say what you *do* know, and how you'd find out the rest. A clean "I haven't used that, but here's how I'd approach it" beats a bluff that gets called. Or, if you confident in your bluffing, by all means, do it.
- **In negotiation:** know your floor before the conversation starts, and be willing to walk away. That's the only real leverage you have, and it comes from [detachment](../3-detachment/README.md).

## Don't go on tilt

Poker players have a word for what happens after a bad loss: **tilt**. They get emotional, chase their losses and make worse decisions. Job seekers tilt too: after a rejection, they lower their standards, freeze up in the next interview. **One hand is one hand.** Good players lose plenty of them. They win because they keep making good decisions over many hands.
