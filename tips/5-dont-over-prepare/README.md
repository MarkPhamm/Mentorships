# 5. Prepare, but don't over-prepare

Preparation matters, up to a point. **After that, there's a diminishing return.** The tenth hour of reviewing the company's history does much less than the first, and somewhere past that it starts to hurt: you sound scripted, you overthink every question, and you walk in more nervous, not less.

Watch [how programmers overprepare for job interviews](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5bId3N7QZec) (Joma Tech). It's a joke, but you'll recognise yourself in it.

## What's worth preparing

- **Your stories.** Two or three projects or experiences you can tell clearly: the problem, what you did, the result. These cover most behavioural questions.
- **The fundamentals for the role.** SQL for analysts and analytics engineers, plus the basics of the stack in the job post.
- **The company and the team.** What they do, what their data team works on, and the people interviewing you (see [Read the people](../2-read-the-people/README.md) and [Coffee chat with the team](../4-coffee-chat-the-team/README.md)).
- **Two or three good questions** to ask them.

## Where to stop

- Memorising word-for-word answers.
- Trying to learn a whole new tool the night before because it's in the "nice to have" list.
- Researching every possible question instead of getting good at thinking out loud.
- Losing sleep. A rested brain beats one more hour of notes.

Prepare enough to be confident, then stop and go in as yourself. **Related:** [Detach](../../mentality/3-detachment/README.md).
