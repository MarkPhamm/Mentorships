# 2. Read the people, more than the role

Every interview has a job description, and every candidate prepares for it. Fewer prepare for **the person across the table.** That's usually what decides it, especially in the early rounds.

## Early rounds: the recruiter

The first call is usually with a recruiter, and **recruiters are usually not technical.** They're checking whether you're a fit on paper, whether you can communicate, and whether a hiring manager will be happy they passed you along.

- **Don't go deep on the tech.** Explaining incremental models and slim CI to a recruiter loses them. Say what you built and the result in plain English: *"I built a pipeline that collected 160,000 airline reviews and turned them into dashboards."*
- **Match their words.** If the job post says "data pipelines", say data pipelines, not "ELT orchestration".
- **Make their job easy.** Clear answers on salary expectations, visa, start date and location. A recruiter who doesn't have to chase you will push you forward.

## Later rounds: read each interviewer

- **Look them up first.** Their LinkedIn tells you their role, background and how technical they are.
- **Adjust as you go.** If they lean in on a detail, go deeper. If their eyes glaze over, zoom out to the result.
- **A hiring manager wants to know you'll make their life easier.** An engineer wants to know you can do the work. A director wants to know you get the business. Same story, told three ways.
