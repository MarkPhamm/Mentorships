# 1. Max out your LinkedIn connections

LinkedIn limits how many connection requests you can send each week. Without Premium, I believe it's around 70, and LinkedIn changes this, so check yours. **Use all of them, every week.** An unused invitation is a free introduction you threw away.

## Who to connect with

**Think about the role you want to be in a few years from now, then connect with the people already in it.** If you want to be an analytics engineer at a fintech, find analytics engineers at fintechs. Their recruiters, their managers, the people who were juniors there two years ago.

- **Genuinely connect.** Read their profile, a post, a project. Connect because you're actually curious about what they do, not to collect a number.
- **Add a short note when you can.** One line on why you're reaching out beats a blank request.
- **Go a level wider than you think.** Recruiters at your target companies, alumni from your school, people in the communities you're part of.

## Turn connections into conversations

**Aim for 1 to 2 coffee chats a week.** A connection is a name in a list. A 20-minute call is someone who remembers you when a role opens, and might refer you. Ask about their work, not for a job: *"I'm working toward an analytics engineering role and I'd love to hear how you got into it."*

Two chats a week is about a hundred conversations a year. That's a network.
